At actual size, [lower coast 667×375](../visual/land-route-area-diagnostic-20261118/hub-lower-coast-667x375-ab.png) shows the orange saturation is lower, but the route becomes a washed-out pale ribbon. Thin clipped strokes around the house and dock remain. [Central stairs 844×390](../visual/land-route-area-diagnostic-20261118/hub-central-stairs-844x390-ab.png) still shows a hard, geometric junction and uniform path body. In both images baseline is left and candidate is right. Neither the small geometry saving nor passing floor tests satisfies the intended natural, interlocked soil/grass/stone composition. The candidate is therefore rejected.

The remaining problem is **authored composition**, not a third choice of ribbon width, opacity or uniform hue. The stone service paving, house apron, stair landing and dock threshold are separate surfaces whose clipping leaves long narrow dirt remnants. A viable revision needs a single local layout plan for the entire lower-court approach: authored paving edges and a physically plausible transition from packed soil to stone and dock boards, with varied soil patches embedded in grass rather than one continuous stripe. Material should vary *within* that local plan (dry compacted center, greener shoulder, scattered embedded stones); applying one pale replacement to every dirt polygon only changes the color of the same shape. Route centerline samples and ground elevation remain acceptance constraints. Do not run a fourth global width, fade, union or band experiment without a new layout drawing and original-size proof.

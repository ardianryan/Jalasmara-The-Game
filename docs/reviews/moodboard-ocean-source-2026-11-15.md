# Ocean source-art follow-up — runtime candidate rejected

Date: 15 November 2026. Branch: `work/moodboard-ocean-source-20261115`, based on `bd96b07`. This report is the only deliverable from this branch. No ocean runtime, texture, generator, import, collision, or progression change is offered for integration or staging.

The [first ocean experiment](moodboard-ocean-2026-11-15.md) failed because a replacement pixel accent read as a slab. I inspected the existing 1536×1024 atmosphere atlas and the native [reef baseline](../visual/ocean-alpha-cut-native-20261115.png). The atlas has painterly porous rock and algae; the playable reef has a wide teal gap to the right of Nala. A deterministic Pillow 11.3 script drew one original 192×160 transparent, six-color, porous karst silhouette. It replaced one of the existing 48 far-plane cards in `OceanBackdrop.gd`, with no added draw card or gameplay data. The trial source used no third-party pixels. The source, raster, and runtime patch have all been removed after review.

The first Godot 4.7.2 Compatibility native run completed six full-scene captures with no `SCRIPT ERROR` or `ERROR`. At 1440×810 its reef image showed essentially no improvement: the new rock fell outside the useful frame. A smaller, repositioned revision was attempted, but native rendering stalled before its first capture for over a minute and was stopped. Thus there is **no** valid 667×375 or 844×390 acceptance proof for that revision. The hand-drawn source preview itself was also too crude against the painted foreground. The candidate is rejected; a passing render command would not establish art quality or moodboard parity.

The next ocean pass should begin with the actual foreground reef/karst geometry and its source textures, matching rock face, edge, shadow, and coral cluster scale before adding distant cards. Keep the central swim route and six resonance targets readable. Capture an identical reef and passage camera at 667×375 and 844×390 before and after; reject a change if it introduces a detached silhouette, square transparency fringe, or markedly different pixel scale. The released alpha-cut ocean remains the baseline until a visually accepted implementation passes integrated world checks and browser review.

# Reef 12 connected-crest diagnostic — 18 November 2026

**Decision: rejected; documentation only.** This isolated branch started at `7c9936a`. No gameplay, renderer, world, collision, coral-mount or test-source change from this trial is intended for `main`.

The defect remains the ruler-straight, dark upper edge of `ReefRelief12` near screen y≈188 at 667×375, below the waste valve. Earlier changes to rear depth, local facets, a small lip, a broad shared cove and inward-only rock faces did not solve it. I tried a connected 17-control-point outcrop that rose 0–72 world units above the existing upper lip, with 42–82 world units of joined face below. It was generated as bounded triangles from the existing source atlas and never changed `WORLD.terrainContours[12]`, boss targets, collision or player resources. The first candidate used the terrain's middle atlas column and the existing mesh/material; the native [667×375](../visual/reef-geometry-solution-20261118/rejected-dark-crest-667x375.png) and [844×390](../visual/reef-geometry-solution-20261118/rejected-dark-crest-844x390.png) frames show that the horizontal edge became a smooth black hill. The line was interrupted, but the new hill is a conspicuous dark obstruction and does not read as porous rock. This is a visual failure.

The second candidate used one extra mesh/material with the brighter limestone atlas column. The native [667×375 frame](../visual/reef-geometry-solution-20261118/rejected-bright-crest-667x375.png) shows recognizable rock texture, but its broad bright face looks like a new floating platform over a black void. That also fails the connected 2.5D geology target. The third trial shortened that face and moved its start right of the swimmer; the native fixture wrote only its shallow 667×375 frame, then spent about 70 seconds on the deep frame without completing. I stopped the process and did not accept an unverified deep result. All three runtime variants and temporary render scripts were reverted or removed.

Godot 4.7.2 Compatibility `ocean-platform06.gd` passed with the first candidate. The first native candidate rendered shallow and deep at both sizes with no `ERROR:` or `SCRIPT ERROR`; the fixture retained oxygen 77, coins 700, and reported its coral mounts. The second candidate rendered at 667×375. A fresh-worktree editor import first crashed at about 79% while rebuilding resources after transient font-cache errors, then passed on retry; later native frames rendered. These are source/native findings only, not browser motion, device performance or full moodboard acceptance. SHA-256 of curated images: dark 667 `0724d77f9e4fd6cf58721a2925b15e98d8245514ec987da28ae7d638898fe7aa`, dark 844 `011436f4c8aa99acadf5ca9b90efb8f39cdb2f583e09220c3aacda5131f22cad`, bright 667 `b748919a69e329ae2685a08237e9cd52d12249e35c7354ca4eadb2c2837b14f9`.

The next solution needs a **composed rock asset or true topological rework** of the shelf and its rear support, with the coral foot locations authored against that same form. A connected outcrop made from simple strips either samples the middle atlas's dark cavern or becomes a decorative bridge with the bright atlas. Do not infer that adding more strip segments or changing extrusion depth alone will solve the composition. Any revised navigable silhouette must use shared world geometry and prove boss target/valve clearance. Existing project atlas and Godot were the only sources/tools in this trial; no external or moodboard pixels entered the game.

## Follow-up: shared slope and narrow fascia

A separate 17-point **authoritative** slope was tested rather than another decorative crest. Its rectangle was enlarged upward to contain the new polygon; a temporary Node test checked bounds, non-crossing edges, blocked shelf cells, the swimmer approach, waste valve and six resonance positions, plus a 40-unit-grid route from the cave approach to each target. All three CPU tests passed, and Godot `ocean-platform06.gd` passed. However the native [667×375 deep frame](../visual/reef-geometry-solution-20261118/rejected-shared-slope-667x375.png) exposes an abrupt vertical left wall under the coral at x≈215, while much of the upper mass remains dark. This is worse than the baseline, so the world data and test were reverted. The failed visual gate is the reason there is no 844×390 shared-slope capture.

The final bounded trial kept the original shared contour and placed a **thin, attached** 13–44-world-unit fascia directly over the existing lip, using the bright source-atlas column in one extra mesh, with no player, collision, coral or boss changes. Both [667×375](../visual/reef-geometry-solution-20261118/rejected-thin-fascia-667x375.png) and [844×390](../visual/reef-geometry-solution-20261118/rejected-thin-fascia-844x390.png) native frames completed. The fascia reads as another straight, pale strip above the old dark band, not natural rock volume; it too was reverted. The focused Godot fixture passed, and no `ERROR:` or `SCRIPT ERROR` appeared in the five focused/native follow-up logs. Render resources remained oxygen 77 and coins 700. The specific remaining composition problem is the **foreground cut edge and its dark face**, not a missing tint. Simple polygons create a straight band or a floating-looking shelf even when route/collision checks pass. A future package should design a coherent porous rock face at game scale and anchor the coral against that face; then update the shared contour and collision to match its actual silhouette. No runtime or test-source change from this follow-up remains.

## What the deep fixture actually shows

`scripts/tests/coral-rock-render.gd` puts both `Camera2D` and the swimmer at world `(4470,2800)` with default zoom `1`. `HybridOcean.gd` gives its orthographic 3D camera the same center and logical viewport height, while `RenderBudget.gd` draws `balanced` at roughly two-thirds resolution and scales the resulting texture nearest-neighbor. Therefore the logical horizontal view is **x=4136.5–4803.5 at 667×375** and **x=4048–4892 at 844×390**. At 667, `WORLD.terrainContours[12]` vertices `(4500,2807)`, `(4630,2800)`, and `(4740,2805)` project near screen `(363.5,194.5)`, `(493.5,187.5)`, and `(603.5,192.5)`. The top segment ending at `(4870,2830)` continues past the right edge. At 844 the same vertices project near `(452,202)`, `(582,195)`, `(692,200)`, while `(4870,2830)` lands near `(822,225)`.

The projection follows from `HybridOcean.gd`'s camera tilt and `TerrainGeometry.gd`'s `_point`: local `(x,-y+sin(12°)z,z)` is under a parent scaled `1/cos(12°)` vertically, and the camera's up vector is `(0,cos(12°),-sin(12°))`. Their dot product is `camera_y-y`; the `z` terms cancel. Thus, before nearest-pixel quantization, screen x is `x-4470+width/2` and screen y is `y-2800+height/2`. Changing only the rear/front **z** depth changes visibility and shading but cannot move the projected edge. The previous rear-depth-only trial's unchanged silhouette is consistent with this geometry.

The shelf is generated as one `ReefRelief12` mesh in `client-godot/scripts/hybrid/TerrainGeometry.gd` `_build_reef` (lines 127–186 at this base). The only source polygon is `packages/shared/src/world.ts` `coastalContour` index 12 (lines 27–38), also used by server swimming collision. The near-horizontal **upper boundary visible at 667** is source vertices 0–5, especially edges 2→3 `(4500,2807)→(4630,2800)`, 3→4 `(4630,2800)→(4740,2805)`, and the visible part of 4→5 `(4740,2805)→(4870,2830)`. `_build_reef` converts those edges into four terrace bands in its segment loop (lines 159–178); the innermost band joins `front`, the polygon inset by `0.09` toward rect center `(5222,2880)`. For example, the source `(4500,2807)` and `(4630,2800)` inset to about `(4565,2814)` and `(4683,2807)`. The contiguous front surface is produced by clipping this inset polygon into three 58-unit horizontal strips and triangulating each strip (lines 144–158). The back triangles (lines 179–181) are farther from the camera. The prior `ReefRelief12` hide-node diagnostic already established this node as the visible mass; source geometry identifies the contributing edge and front loops, although individual winning GPU triangle IDs were not captured.

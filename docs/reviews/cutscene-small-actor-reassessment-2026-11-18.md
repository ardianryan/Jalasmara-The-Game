# Phase 1 cutscene actor-size reassessment at 667×375

The previous [wide-shot audit](cutscene-wide-actor-audit-2026-11-18.md) accepted these frames because a **stage actor or portrait** could identify the speaker. That rule was too permissive for the user's concern: people acting in the scene can still look miniature while a large portrait carries the dialogue. I re-read the same twelve original-size native stills. This is a stricter visual assessment, not a new render, motion test, source change, or claim of full moodboard parity. The captures are from source `1db661e`; any later animation change must be checked anew.

Estimated heights below are visible stage-actor ink at 667×375, excluding shadow, dialogue portrait and decorative frame. They are approximate visual measurements from stills, not automated segmentation. “Fail” means the body action is hard to read at the actual viewport even when the dialogue portrait is clear.

The **worst story beat is `victory:3`** because the player's reward depends on reading the children and residents' joy. A useful next trial is a two-plane dock blocking pass: keep the harbor and sampan as the establishing plate, then use a short foreground dock cut-in where Beno and two children cross into the near plane with distinct jump/clap poses and visible feet on the deck. Return to the wide shot for the light rising over town. This changes focal framing and staging, **not** every actor's scale constant. Each near-plane pose needs its own inspected pixel art or hand-authored connected sprite; enlarging the existing 64 px source uniformly would expose blocky limbs and crowd the frame. Capture motion and stills at 667×375 and 844×390 before acceptance.

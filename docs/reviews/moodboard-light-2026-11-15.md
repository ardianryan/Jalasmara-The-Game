# Land lighting: daylight, sunset, and night

15 November 2026. Local candidate on `work/moodboard-light-20261115` from `06e0a82`. This package is restricted to the land renderer's environment and directional light. It does not change materials, texture resolution, geometry, camera, UI, character art, gameplay, or the server-owned 36-minute clock.

The previous renderer mapped daylight directly between teal night and cream daytime. It had no distinct sunset key light. The new palette retains a warm cream daytime sun, adds a bounded coral sunset highlight only during the clock's `senja` phase, and keeps ambient light blue-teal so unlit surfaces remain cool. Outdoor night ambient energy moves from `0.50` to `0.54`; home stays at `0.65`. The sunset influence fades to zero at full day and full night. The existing light, grade shader, shadow settings, four-Hz weather update, and quality profile are unchanged. The initial light state now matches the first full-day update to avoid a one-frame flash.

Source and provenance: all values are original project color choices informed by the approved moodboard, not colors sampled from or copied out of external images. No asset or third-party runtime was added; project code license remains applicable. The native capture fixture saves paired old/new 844×390 frames for hub and forest at 12:00, 17:30, and 20:00 with the same camera and quality profile. It writes output under `artifacts/godot/moodboard-light/`, which is a local test artifact rather than a distributable asset.

Verification used Godot 4.7.2 Compatibility on Apple M2. The first fresh import logged missing transient fontdata before the import finished; a second `--editor --quit` pass had no `ERROR` or `SCRIPT ERROR` (`.runtime/light-import2.log`). `scripts/tests/land-lighting-palette.gd` passed (`.runtime/light-palette.log`). `scripts/tests/land-lighting-render.gd` produced 12 paired 844×390 screenshots, same camera and clock for each pair, without `ERROR` or `SCRIPT ERROR` (`.runtime/light-render.log`). The paired native results are local in `artifacts/godot/moodboard-light/` and are not part of the source commit. This is a native Mac render, not a Windows/Samsung GPU or public-browser benchmark.

Visual review accepted the bounded result: midday is unchanged, 17:30 has warmer amber roads and timber while the shadowed greens stay cool, and 20:00 exposes paths modestly better without washing out warm lanterns. The full-frame mean luminance at dusk changed from 107.48 to 109.65 in hub and 111.41 to 113.58 in forest. Night changed from 47.23 to 53.19 in hub and 45.41 to 51.64 in forest; those measurements support visibility only, not accessibility or gameplay comfort.

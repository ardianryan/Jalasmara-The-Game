# Village stepping and pixel-density audit, 12 November 2026

The owner reports that villagers look like still pictures sliding between places. A small, readable alternating step is sufficient; they do not need Nala's full physical gait. The owner also finds the land scene blurry and asks whether the existing pixel-grid scanner can accelerate the fix. This is a source and offline-tool audit only: no runtime, asset, export or staging change was made, and no moving game capture was taken here.

## What the current code does

- `HubActor.gd` supplies schedules, routes, movement velocity and `NpcAnimationState` clips. However, `_activity_update()` returns before walking whenever the player is within 165 logical units. It also sends residents to rest outside work hours and to shelter when rain exceeds 0.42. Thus a nearby player can observe a station pose or halted travel for a long time. Proximity alone is broader than the actual dialogue pause.
- `LandActors3D.gd` gives some villagers articulated bodies: Ujang, Darmi and Sari use `NpcBodyRig3D`; the director and five children use `VillageResidentBody3D` or the reviewed resident-art path. The remaining portrait-only villagers use one atlas frame for the whole body. `VillageActivity3D` can animate their work props, but the fallback body frame is unchanged during travel. That path visually slides even though its logical position and `walk` clip advance. Existing route and clip tests do not prove a visible alternating step on that fallback path.
- The Godot UI layout starts at 960×540, while `RenderBudget.gd` and `LandWorld3D.gd` render the 3D land to 480×270 at 16:9 with a 129,600-pixel budget and nearest upscaling. For a 1170×540 wide logical viewport the budget yields about 529×244. At 1920×1080, a 480×270 land frame is enlarged 4×. The `Ringan` and `Seimbang` settings currently change shadows but share this pixel budget. These numbers explain coarse or muddy detail; they do not by themselves prove a texture-filter bug. `LandMaterials.gd` and the land display already request nearest sampling.

## Offline scanner trial

The installed `@xprite/pixelate` 0.1.2 passed its fixture check (`npm run check --prefix tools/xprite-pixelate`). Pillow 11.3.0 supplied RGBA bytes from two original project assets. Scanning the complete 288×256 `village-expansion-v1/residents.png` atlas returned `reliable=false`, confidence 0. Scanning the first 512×512 cell of `village-residents-v1.png` returned a nominal 172×166 grid, approximately 3-pixel cells, but confidence was only 0.0377. Neither result is trustworthy enough for automatic production replacement. The separately installed Pixel Snapper adapter was verified at upstream commit `02f9315d` (MIT); it was not run on production images. No recovered image was saved or promoted; temporary RGBA inputs were deleted. A quick offline attempt to warp a whole lower body and then to shift isolated foot crops produced visible tears and detached feet. Both preview images were discarded and deleted. The scanners can help review an individual upscaled portrait's grid, but cannot create missing walk poses or add detail to a 480×270 3D render. Image-to-Pixel remains an optional palette authoring path, not a runtime fix.

## Next bounded package

1. Capture a named portrait-only resident walking beside Nala on the active build, plus one articulated resident as a control, at known clock/weather/quality. Record actual position, clip, body renderer, viewport size and frames; distinguish station time from failed movement.
2. Give portrait-only residents a deliberately simple two- or four-pose step: alternating planted feet, a small opposing arm/body sway and stable head/foot pivot. Use reviewed source cutouts with consistent atlas coordinates; preserve their clothing, age and portrait identity. The rejected full-body warp and detached-foot crop must not be promoted. Do not process the whole atlas with a grid tool or substitute unrelated sprites. Keep static work props separate.
3. Replace the broad proximity-only travel freeze with a dialogue-specific pause while preserving the player's ability to interact with a moving resident. Keep schedule, collision, shelter, reduced-motion and save behavior. Test walking past a resident without talking, starting dialogue, and resuming afterward.
4. Compare the current 480×270 land render with an optional sharper 640×360 16:9 profile using the same camera and time, with nearest sampling and pixel alignment. Retain the current budget for `Ringan`; do not raise the S23 default before physical frame-time and battery/heat checks. Review actual-size screenshots and movement for crispness and shimmer. Only use Xprite/Pixel Snapper on source images that prove a grid-recovery problem.

Acceptance requires a moving capture with visibly alternating feet on a portrait-only villager, interaction and collision preserved, and a side-by-side actual-size sharpness/performance comparison on Windows and S23. Source tests and a single still cannot substitute for either visual gate. The active staging audio release remains unchanged.

# Land color and continuous daylight candidate — 15 November 2026

This local candidate addresses two visible problems: muted land materials at game scale and a hard environmental tint change when the clock crosses the night flag. Branch `work/moodboard-shader-20261115` starts from `aa41699fe86b4befc64d9a00d5f8640c386ae03d`. It has not been merged, pushed, accepted by CI, or activated on staging. The existing server-owned 36-minute clock is unchanged.

`LandWorld3D` now derives sunset color from the continuous minute of day rather than the discrete `senja` phase. Sun, ambient, lantern and grade values use elapsed-time exponential smoothing on every frame; the shadow's opacity approaches zero before the shadow pass switches off. `EnvironmentEffects` blends its tint continuously from day to night instead of selecting a different target on the `night` flag. `EnvironmentState.snapshot()` exposes its already-smoothed `visual_minutes` for rendering only. This does not grant time or gameplay progress on the client.

Land materials use slightly warmer soil, livelier tropical grass, and cooler stone sides. A land-only single-sample grade split-tones shadows toward teal and highlights toward cream. It replaces the prior land grade; the ocean keeps its existing shader. Microtexture contrast is quantized without changing the source pixel grid. No blur, bloom, new light, pass, material instance, collision, asset or third-party dependency was added. The paired fixture recorded the same material counts for baseline and candidate: 11 for the hub, 23 for the forest. This is a source-level draw/material check, **not** a GPU frame-time benchmark; per-frame lighting uniforms and lantern updates need browser and device profiling after integration.

Native Godot 4.7.2 Compatibility produced 24 paired baseline/candidate frames at 844×390 and 667×375 across hub/forest and day/dusk/night (`artifacts/godot/moodboard-land-material/`). The baseline material shader was extracted from the pinned base commit. The first paired fixture sampled its night frame during the 0.5-second transition from dusk, so its night screenshot was rejected. The corrected fixture settles each clock keyframe for 2.5 seconds and restores all shader uniforms when switching between source variants. Its rerun passed without `ERROR` or `SCRIPT ERROR`. At 667×375, mean absolute RGB difference was 7.59/5.85/2.87 in hub day/dusk/night and 10.56/7.87/3.56 in forest day/dusk/night; these are change magnitudes, not quality scores. Actual-size review found a clearer warm path and greener foliage by day, a warmer dusk, and legible cool stone at night. The improvement is moderate; it does **not** establish full moodboard parity.
